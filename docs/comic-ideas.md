# Comic backlog

Seeds for the programmer-art page. Captured Sept 8, 2026, the afternoon the page shipped, while the material was still fresh. Not drawn yet. The three below all came out of a single session and they share a subject: the model being competent, correct, and standing outside the joke.

Cast is the one already established on the page: the robot, and the human with the coffee.

## 1. Wait, was my mic on?

The one that happened. I typed a message that performed dictation: a stream of instructions with a self-correction mid-sentence ("add, no don't that last part") and then, at the end, "wait, was my mic on?" It was typed on purpose. Nobody asks that in text.

The agent parsed it correctly. It honored the retraction, did not add the line I had cancelled, shipped the edit, and then told me plainly: **yes, your mic was on.**

Panel: the human at a keyboard, speech bubble reading "wait, was my mic on?" The robot, helpful and untroubled: "Yes."

This is the strongest of the three because it has a victim and the victim is not the human.

## 2. Everything is a ticket

The same failure one level up. The human says something conversational, performative, or absurd, and it comes back as a parsed work item with the ambiguous parts flagged for review. The joke is the reflex: there is no register in which a thing said is not a thing to be done.

Panel: the human makes an offhand remark. The robot is already holding a filed, well-formatted issue with a checklist.

## 3. The receipt

The page says, in its own words, that what the model could not do was tell when a drawing had become funny. The model demonstrated this on that page, on the same afternoon, to the person who wrote the line.

Panel: the robot presenting that sentence proudly on the easel, while directly behind it the demonstration is underway and it has not turned around.

This one is the closest to the recursion comic already on the page and might be the same joke twice. Check before drawing.

## 4. Is this just a me thing?

Held, weaker, but true and it happened the same day. The site would not resolve in my browser. The agent diagnosed it with total confidence across four public resolvers, and the cause was that the agent had poisoned my resolver's negative cache hours earlier by checking whether the domain was available, before I had bought it.

Panel: the robot presenting a clean four-point diagnosis. Small print at the bottom: cause of problem, see above.
